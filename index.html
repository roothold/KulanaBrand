My Story

From Freedom to Purpose

My story did not begin with wealth, connections, or opportunity handed to me. It began with an immigrant family, sacrifice, and a belief that freedom itself was an extraordinary gift.

The gift of freedom

I grew up in Malaysia in a large family. My parents opened their lives and their home to many children, raising nearly eighteen between their own and those they adopted. Eventually we migrated to the United States in search of something my parents believed was more valuable than material wealth: freedom and opportunity.

We did not come with much. But they gave us something that became the foundation of my life. They told us that circumstances may determine where you begin, but they do not have to determine where you finish.

"The only thing I can give you is your freedom. If you want to become somebody in this world, you choose your path and you do it for yourself."

My parents

An entrepreneur before I knew the word

As a teenager, I worked wherever I could. My parents produced clothing in bulk, and I saw an opportunity in the unique sample pieces — I began selling them to friends. It was simple, but it was my first taste of building something. I discovered I could look at something ordinary and see what it could become.

I studied psychology, business, and information technology, and later began graduate studies while working full-time. I have never followed a path simply because everyone else followed it.

Five offers, and one decision

Out of college I received five job offers and entered the telecommunications industry — the corporate world many of my classmates dreamed of. Within months I advanced to a director-level position, extraordinarily early. It should have felt like I had arrived. Instead, I realized I didn't want someone else deciding how high I was allowed to climb. So I walked away.

Building from the ground up

In my early twenties I started Granite Design Stone Company. Over the years I have founded roughly ten companies organically — no institutional investors, no large loans. I learned industries, identified opportunities, built relationships, solved problems, and created businesses from nothing.

When I entered insurance in 2011 and announced I wanted to become an FMO, people laughed. They said it takes twenty years. I have heard "you can't do that yet" my entire life. My answer has almost always been the same: Why not?

The connector

Throughout my life, people have called me the connector. I connect people, friends, businesses, partners — sometimes people who would never have found one another. When you have access to relationships, knowledge, or opportunity, you should not hold those things tightly. You should use them to create opportunities for others. I have always been a listener. I listened. I encouraged. I tried to lift people up.

When my own world changed

One of the greatest trials of my life came through the unexpected breakdown of my marriage. For someone who had spent her entire adult life solving problems and helping others through crises, I encountered something I could not solve with another business plan. For several months, I stopped — and for me, stopping was almost unimaginable.

What initially felt like the darkest period of my life became one of the most revealing. I prayed more. I meditated. I spent time alone. I learned things about myself that decades of constant activity had never allowed me to see. Most importantly, I became closer to God. My trials did not eliminate my ambition. They gave my ambition a clearer purpose.

"Strength is not simply the ability to keep moving. Sometimes it is the courage to become still long enough to hear what God is trying to tell you."

Kulana

Beauty from the inside out

For years I have been drawn to beauty — not simply appearances, but the process of transforming something into the best version of itself. I could walk into a space and imagine what it could become. Eventually I realized I felt the same way about people. That idea became part of my vision for Qulana Beauty.

Beauty, to me, begins inside. It is caring for your skin and your body, but also your mind, your character, and your spirit. I believe our bodies are temples entrusted to us by God. Real beauty is physical, emotional, and spiritual.

A voice for other women

Today another unexpected chapter is unfolding: I am becoming a writer. I am writing and co-writing several books on entrepreneurship, womanhood, resilience, and rebuilding — including The Woman They Tried to Erase, Women Entrepreneurs, and Ex-Wives of Texas. They are connected by one theme: what happens to a woman after betrayal, and who she chooses to become afterward.

I don't want to tell stories only about what people did to one another. I want to talk about what happens next — how a woman protects her faith, her family, her future, and rediscovers who she is.

"I don't want to speak to women from the finish line. I want to walk beside them on the road back."

Kulana

The woman they couldn't erase

You can lose a relationship, money, a company, the future you thought you were going to have. But none of those things has the authority to determine your worth. The woman underneath the titles and accomplishments is still there — and perhaps she is stronger, wiser, more compassionate, and more purposeful than the woman who existed before.

You can be successful and still be hurt. You can be strong and still need help. You can lose the life you planned without losing your purpose. And you can wake up believing your story is over, only to discover God was preparing you to write the next chapter yourself.

Faith & Philanthropy

Success creates an obligation to reach back

I have never been motivated solely by accumulating things. When I imagine a successful company, I immediately imagine what it could make possible: a village helped, a child educated, a family housed, a person without a home given a new beginning.

Housing & dignity

New approaches to affordable housing and communities that restore what homelessness takes away: dignity, stability, belonging, and hope. A home is more than four walls — it is somewhere a person can begin again.

Children & seniors

A lifelong commitment to children, orphans, seniors, and underserved communities — and to people who simply need an opportunity to rebuild their lives.

Faith at the center

My relationship with God shapes the decisions I make and the person I want to become. Caring for ourselves and each other extends far beyond what anyone sees in the mirror.

"Freedom creates opportunity. Opportunity creates responsibility. And success creates an obligation to reach back and help someone else rise."
